# What it costs

Money is the one thing a learner must never discover afterwards. When a card describes something that can bill — a managed service, a cloud resource, a paid tier — say so on that card:

"cost": "Free while it runs locally. A managed instance of this starts at around $9 a month."

The app draws it as its own panel under the card, with a coin, so a learner who skims the prose cannot skim past the price.

- **Real numbers where you have them**, and the shape of the charge: per hour, per month, per request. "It can get expensive" is not a cost line.
- **Only where money is genuinely in play**, and that is the whole bar. A lesson about an idea has no cost line anywhere. A lesson that has the learner create something on a paid service has one on every card that creates something.
- **"It is free" is worth saying only where a reader would assume otherwise** — a tool with a well-known paid tier, a cloud service with a free allowance. Never as reassurance on a lesson nobody suspected of costing anything: a cost panel that keeps saying "free" teaches the learner to stop reading cost panels, and the one that says $9/month then reads like all the others.
- When a whole lesson needs an account or a card on file, say it at the start, not at step nine.
